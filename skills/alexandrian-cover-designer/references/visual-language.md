# Alexandrian graphical cover language

Use this as a design system, not as a template. Every cover must remain an
original interpretation of its own book.

## Shared qualities

- One dominant symbolic image should carry the cover at thumbnail size. Give
  it roughly half or more of the visual weight; do not substitute a field of
  thin diagram lines.
- Use deep black or warm paper as a decisive ground, then organize a small
  number of substantial color masses. Jewel cobalt, stained-glass amber,
  forest green, bone, brass, vermilion, or safety orange may serve as one
  book-specific accent system.
- Make surfaces tactile: xerox grain, halftone dots, torn paper, oxidized
  metal, stained glass, screenprint ink, analog film bloom, archival cards,
  or imperfect registration.
- Allow surreal scale, impossible perspective, or one unexpected splice when
  it sharpens the book's central tension.
- Preserve calm regions for live HTML title, quotation, and edition. These
  regions may still contain low-contrast material texture; they should not
  feel like an empty default canvas.

## Available directions

Choose one primary direction and, at most, one supporting direction.

1. **Surreal spatial allegory** — monumental architecture or objects around a
   very small human-scale witness; severe void, vertiginous depth, luminous
   focal event.
2. **Illuminated narrative** — rich stained-glass or mosaic color, iconic
   silhouette, dense story-bearing shapes, architectural framing. Use secular
   imagery unless the book itself requires otherwise.
3. **Xerox editorial montage** — high-contrast photocopy, halftone, torn
   fragments, one forceful central image, disciplined asymmetric panels. The
   generated art stays text-free because typography is live in the frontend.
4. **Retro-futurist field document** — speculative machinery or interfaces
   joined to landscape, biology, or archival material; precise technical
   detail interrupted by one lush or luminous organic field.

## Originality and safety boundary

- Reference images are mood-only. Never reproduce their composition,
  characters, slogans, typography, religious scenes, anatomy, or recognizable
  artwork.
- Do not use giant eyes, crucifixion imagery, skeletons, angels, dragons, or a
  familiar poster layout merely because a reference contains them.
- Never render readable words, logos, labels, numbers, equations, or a title
  inside the artwork. Do not simulate illegible pseudo-text as a focal motif.
- Do not turn a visual metaphor into evidence, operating guidance, or a claim
  that the finalized book does not make.

## Shelf-level variation

Read the wrapper-supplied prior-cover inventory. Avoid repeating another
cover's dominant object, camera angle, silhouette, or primary direction. A
shared material language should make the shelf coherent; subject, spatial
logic, and accent palette should make each volume distinct.

Treat the two most recent covers as the strict comparison set. Derive the new
dominant symbol from the current book's exact core question, and state the
specific recent objects or compositions being avoided. Variation is not a
random palette swap: the intellectual question must change the cover's main
object and spatial argument.
